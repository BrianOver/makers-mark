Recommendations for Maker’s Mark

Overall Impression

You are much closer to a genuinely new game than it first appears. The
core idea is not “cozy crafting.” The core idea is being emotionally
invested in autonomous AI characters through craftsmanship instead of
combat.

The real loop is: Observe -> Understand -> Craft -> Watch -> Feel

The “watch” phase is the unique part of the experience.

Major Recommendation

The goal should not simply be to give the player more things to do
during the middle of the day.

Instead ask: “How do we make the player unable to look away?”

Watching heroes should itself be engaging because the player is
emotionally invested.

Heroes Become “Your” Heroes

Over time, players should stop thinking: “The Paladin”

and instead think: “Oh thank God Sarah survived.”

Relationships should become the primary long-term progression, more
important than recipes or buildings.

Hero Personality

Heroes should have memorable quirks: - Refuses potions - Loves ugly
equipment - Always bargains - Never bargains - Only buys blue gear -
Loyal to your shop despite weaker equipment

Players remember personalities more than optimization.

Failure Creates Stories

Never erase mistakes.

Instead let failure create: - Memorials - Scars - Retirements -
Heirlooms - Apprentices - Legends

Example: A hero loses an arm because of poor armor, then later returns
smiling and asks for another shield.

Heroes Should Surprise Players

Heroes should not always optimize.

Sometimes they choose gear because: - It’s lucky. - It belonged to
family. - They simply like it.

This reinforces that they are people rather than algorithms.

Emotional Loop

Observe Hero Understand Hero Help Hero Watch Outcome Remember Story Care
More Tomorrow

Avoid reducing the game to: Craft -> Money -> Upgrade

Preserve Uncertainty

Avoid exposing perfect survival percentages.

Instead present information like: “This looks dangerous.”

Allow players to interpret situations.

Maintain Hero Agency

The player enables success rather than controlling it.

Heroes still deserve credit for surviving.

Letters

One of the strongest possible additions is tiny personal letters.

Examples:

“Your shield held. Thought you’d like to know.”

“The potion tasted awful. Worked though.”

These do more for attachment than long dialogue trees.

Town Reaction

Let the town react naturally.

Examples: - Baker comments on a hero you helped. - Children admire
famous weapons. - Tavern patrons discuss successful expeditions.

Living Item Histories

Continue expanding item provenance.

Example history: Forged Day 3 Killed Goblin King Saved Sarah Reforged
after death Inherited by apprentice Retired

These histories become collectibles in themselves.

The Window

A potentially iconic feature:

Each morning heroes physically walk past your shop toward adventure.

Players watch them leave wearing equipment they crafted.

Eventually the player worries about specific heroes returning.

This simple recurring scene reinforces the entire emotional premise.

Biggest Design Rule

Whenever considering a new mechanic ask:

“Does this make me care more about a specific hero?”

If the answer is no, it is probably secondary.

Suggested Vision Statement

Maker’s Mark is a cozy simulation where you never become the hero—you
become the craftsman whose work shapes legends, one relationship at a
time.
